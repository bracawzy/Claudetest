How it works

If variable costs are 60% of your price, each sale leaves you a 40% margin.

Discount: a 20% discount cuts that margin in half, to 20%, so you need to sell twice as many units (+100%) just to make the same profit. The formula is more units = discount ÷ (margin − discount). If the discount is as big as the margin, every sale loses money and no amount of extra sales can make up for it.

Price increase: a 20% increase lifts the margin to 60%, so you can sell a third fewer units (−33%) and still make the same profit. The formula is units you can lose = increase ÷ (margin + increase).

In both cases, margin = 100% − variable costs.

Based on the break-even examples in Hermann Simon's Power Pricing.
